Abstract

Project summary

Deep learning models for thoracic disease classification from chest radiographs achieve strong diagnostic performance, yet their spatial reasoning remains opaque. Post-hoc methods such as Grad-CAM generate attention maps only at inference time and are not optimised to align with clinical findings. We propose a variant CNN that incorporates explanation supervision directly into training: a spatial attention module (A ∈ ℝ1×7×7) is jointly trained to classify 14 thoracic pathologies and to place its activation on clinician-annotated bounding boxes via an auxiliary loss Lattn. A label co-occurrence regulariser Lcorr further constrains predictions to respect clinical co-morbidity patterns. Evaluated on the NIH ChestX-ray14 benchmark (112,120 images, 880 bounding-box test cases), our ResNet-50 variant achieves a macro AUC of 0.7603 and an IoU of 0.2991, representing a +34.7 % relative localisation gain over post-hoc Grad-CAM at zero additional inference cost. DenseNet-121 attains the highest IoU of 0.3178. Statistical significance was confirmed via DeLong's test (p < 3 × 10−31) across all 14 pathology classes.

Method

Architecture, loss functions, and training protocol

CNN Backbone

ImageNet-pretrained encoder. Produces 7×7 feature map F. Three variants tested: ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B0.

Spatial Attention (core)

1×1 Conv → sigmoid. Produces A ∈ ℝ^(1×7×7). Attended features: F' = F̃ ⊙ A. Trained jointly via L_attn to align with bounding boxes.

Channel Attention (CBAM)

Squeeze-excitation over channels. Optional module; empirically aids classification without penalising localisation.

L_cls — Classification

Focal loss (γ=2) combined with inverse-frequency weighted BCE. Handles the severe label imbalance across all 14 NIH classes.

L_attn — Explanation Supervision

BCE between upsampled 7×7 attention map and bounding-box mask. Primary driver of IoU improvement (+34.7%). Applied only to annotated samples.

L_corr — Co-occurrence

Penalises clinically implausible label combinations using a precomputed co-occurrence matrix. Recovers ~1.4% AUC with no spatial cost.

The total training loss combines three terms, each serving a distinct purpose in the learning objective:

Lcls
FL(p,y) = −α(1−pt)γlog(pt)
+ wc · BCE(ŷ, y)

Focal loss (γ=2, α=0.25) downweights easy negatives. Class weight w_c = N_neg/N_pos handles imbalance.

Lattn ★ Key
Lattn = BCE(↑A, Mbox)

where ↑A = upsample(A, 224)

Binary cross-entropy between the upsampled 7×7 attention map and the bounding-box binary mask. Applied only to the 880 annotated samples per epoch.

Lcorr
Lcorr = ∑i≠j Cij · ŷi · (1−ŷj)

Penalises predictions that violate the training-set co-occurrence matrix C. Encourages co-occurring pathologies to be jointly detected.

Total loss: L = Lcls + λattn · Lattn + λcorr · Lcorr     λattn = 2.0,   λcorr = 0.3    (selected on validation AUC + IoU Pareto front)

Reading: Lattn alone drives the localisation gain (+31.7% IoU) at the cost of AUC (−10.9%). Adding Lcorr recovers +1.4% AUC (0.7512 → 0.7603) without any spatial trade-off, confirming its role as a pure classification regulariser. The full model is the Pareto-optimal operating point on the AUC–IoU frontier.
